# Raw findings — W3C WAI accessible presentations

Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/teach-advocate/accessible-presentations/

Credibility: Tier 1 — W3C Web Accessibility Initiative.

Relevant extracted findings:

- Accessible sessions are good general practice and particularly important for cognitive accessibility.
- For meetings and presentations: start with an overview and end with a review of the most important points.
- Use consistent design in slide presentations to limit cognitive load.
- Use clear and understandable content.
- Offer handouts, slides, and other material in accessible formats; HTML material should meet WCAG at least Level AA.
- Screen sharing content is often not accessible; provide material so participants can access it directly.
- Limit the amount of text on each slide because many people cannot read text and listen to the speaker at the same time.
- Make text and important visuals big enough to be read from the back of the room.
- Use easy-to-read fonts; thin/fancy fonts are harder to read from a distance.
- Use sufficient luminance contrast for text/background and graph colors.
- Consider room conditions: dark text on light background in light rooms; light text on dark background in dark rooms.
- Avoid unnecessary motion; avoid blinking/flashing.
- During presentation, describe all relevant visual information and do not rely on “you can read it on the slide.”
- Speak clearly, avoid speaking too fast, and use simple language.
