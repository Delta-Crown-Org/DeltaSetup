# Raw findings — premium aesthetics and motion

Sources:
- Nielsen Norman Group, Aesthetic-Usability Effect: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/
- Material Design 3 Motion: https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/overview

Accessed: 2026-05-17

## NN/g aesthetic-usability effect

- Users tend to perceive attractive products as more usable.
- A positive emotional response to visual design makes users more tolerant of minor usability issues.
- Attractive design helps a site appear orderly, well-designed, and professional.
- The effect has limits: visual polish cannot compensate for major usability problems or sacrificing function for aesthetics.
- Researchers should watch what users do, not just what they say, because visual appeal can mask usability issues in feedback.

## Material motion guidance

- Current Material guidance frames motion as a system with consistent schemes/tokens.
- Expressive motion is appropriate for hero/key interactions; standard motion feels more functional.
- Spatial motion applies to x/y position, rotation, size, and rounded corners.
- Effects motion applies to opacity/color and should avoid overshoot.
- Most motion should use the same scheme; custom/specific moments can swap schemes intentionally.
- Smaller elements may move faster; larger/full-screen transitions should move slower.

## Deck implications

Premium deck polish is valuable because executives infer professionalism and order from aesthetics. However, it must support comprehension. Use motion tokens consistently, reserve expressive motion for key narrative transformations, and keep functional/status information stable and readable.
