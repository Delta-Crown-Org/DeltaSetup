# Raw Findings — Journey Mapping and Assertion-Evidence Narrative

Sources:
- NN/g Journey Mapping 101: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/

## NN/g journey mapping findings

- Definition: a journey map visualizes the process a person goes through to accomplish a goal.
- Basic process: compile user actions into a timeline, add thoughts and emotions to create a narrative, then condense and polish into a visualization.
- Common key elements:
  1. Actor
  2. Scenario + expectations
  3. Journey phases
  4. Actions, mindsets, and emotions
  5. Opportunities
- One point of view per map creates a stronger, clearer narrative.
- Journey maps are best for scenarios with a sequence of events, transitions over time, or multiple channels.
- Journey maps support shared understanding: they force conversation, align mental models, and communicate concise memorable shared vision.
- Experience maps are broader than journey maps; they describe generic human experiences rather than a specific product/service scenario.
- Service blueprints are business/internal-process extensions of journey maps; for this deck, avoid drifting into blueprint/architecture detail.

## Assertion-evidence findings

- Common slide default of phrase headline plus bullet list creates noise and makes it hard for audiences to know where to look.
- Assertion-evidence approach uses succinct message headlines (assertions) rather than phrase topics.
- Assertions are supported by visual evidence rather than bullet lists.

Deck implications:

- Use assertion headlines for every slide.
- Use the operator as the actor and keep one point of view.
- Use a journey map as proof of experience impact, not as a dense workshop artifact.
- Avoid service-blueprint details unless placed in appendix/handout for implementation teams.
